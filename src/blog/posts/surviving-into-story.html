I believe some of the most powerful stories are born from the things we never thought we’d survive. That is not a slogan I arrived at cleverly. It is the plain description of how every book I have written came to exist — late, and only after the season it belonged to had finally loosened its grip.

There is a long distance between living through a chapter and being able to write it. In the middle of a hard season, there is no narrative. There is only Tuesday, and then Wednesday. You are not gathering material. You are not noticing the good details. You are holding a life together with both hands, and the part of you that makes meaning has gone quiet because meaning is not what the moment requires. What the moment requires is that you keep going.

So the writing comes afterward. Sometimes long afterward. You look back at a stretch of time you could not describe while you were inside it, and you find that it has a shape. A beginning you did not choose. A middle you barely remember. And an end that arrived not as rescue but as the slow return of ordinary things — sleep, appetite, the ability to be interested in someone else’s day.

That shape is what I mean by story. Not a tidy lesson. Not an explanation that makes the pain make sense, because some pain does not agree to be explained. Just a shape: this happened, and then this, and here is who was left standing.

I write from the heart about faith, healing, loss, purpose, and finding yourself again, and those five words are not categories on a shelf. They are the order things tend to happen in. Faith is what you are left holding when the plan is gone. Healing is slower and less linear than anyone warns you. Loss keeps its own calendar and does not consult yours. Purpose grows underground for a long time before it shows. And finding yourself again is the last one, because it is the hardest — the person who comes out of a hard season is not the person who went in, and there is grief in that too, even when the new person is stronger.

The novels and the devotionals do this work differently. In The Price Series I can put the question inside a character and let her carry it further than I could. Kathryn gets to say out loud what most of us only think: that survival is not the finish line, that the body continuing is not the same as a life being lived. In Rooted in Purpose and 365 Days of Grace, there is no character to hide behind. There is just the page, and the attempt to say something true without dressing it up.

Either way the aim is the same. My words are rooted in real experience and in the belief that even our most painful chapters can become part of something beautiful — not because the pain was secretly good, but because something on the other side of it turned out to be worth having.

If you are in the middle of a chapter right now and you cannot see the shape of it, that is not a failure of faith or of attention. You are not supposed to be able to see it yet. The seeing comes later. For now, the work is simply to get through, and to trust that this will be legible someday, even if not today.

And if something I write makes even one person feel seen, understood, or a little less alone, then every word was worth it. That has always been the whole measure.
