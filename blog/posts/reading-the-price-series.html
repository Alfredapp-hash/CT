How to read The Price Series

The question I am asked most often about these three novels is the simplest one: where do I start? The answer is that the books are meant to be read in order — The Price of Choosing You, then The Price of Letting Go, then What We Keep. Not because the plot would confuse you otherwise, though it might, but because the three books are asking three different questions, and each one only becomes a real question once the previous one has been answered.

Book One: The Price of Choosing You

Kathryn wakes in the dark with her wrists bound, and the first thing she has to learn is that the man holding the key is not the worst thing looking for her. That single inversion is the engine of the book. She was not being held from the world. She was being held against it — and the distinction is not comfort. It is a second cage built around the first.

Book One is a novel of survival and sacrifice: the rules of silence, the cost of mercy, and the choice she has to make when fear changes shape. Notice that word, changes. Fear does not leave in this book. It simply stops pointing where it used to point, and Kathryn has to make a decision while the ground under it is still moving. What she chooses here sets the price that the other two books collect.

Book Two: The Price of Letting Go

Three months free, living under a borrowed name in a borrowed house, and the quiet still feels wrong. This is the book readers do not expect, and it is the one I most wanted to write. Escape stories usually end at the door. Book Two begins on the other side of it, in the strange flat weather of a life that is technically safe and does not yet feel like anyone’s.

The question shifts from can she get out to what does it cost to stay out. There are the names that still matter. There is the lie between her and Kalem, which is not a plot device so much as a load-bearing wall. And there is the moment Kathryn stops disappearing — stops making herself smaller and quieter as a survival habit, and begins to take up the space a free person takes up. Freedom, in this book, still has teeth.

Book Three: What We Keep

After the story is public, Kathryn learns that truth does not quiet the world. It amplifies it. Book Three is the trial, the aftermath, and the slower work underneath both: legacy, healing, the names worth keeping, and a life finally shaped by choice instead of captivity.

“I used to think surviving was the finish line,” she said. “But survival is just the body continuing. Living requires choice.”

That line is the reason the series is three books and not one. Survival is where most stories about a woman in danger end, and it is genuinely the smallest thing that happens to Kathryn. Everything that matters — what she keeps, who she becomes, what she is willing to pay for a life she actually wants — happens after the danger is over.

A note for readers who need one

These novels deal with captivity, coercion, and violence. They are not gratuitous, but they are not gentle either. If you are looking for something quieter from the same set of concerns — the cost of freedom, the work of becoming yourself again — Finding Your Self-Worth asks many of the same questions in a room with the lights on.
